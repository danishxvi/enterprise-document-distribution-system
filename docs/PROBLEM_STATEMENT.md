# Problem statement

## Context

Large organisations issue a constant stream of official documents: circulars
that carry policy and guidance, orders that record postings and sanctions, and
notifications that announce time sensitive events. These documents need to reach
the right people and, just as importantly, stay findable long after they were
first sent.

In many organisations this whole process runs on email and shared drives. That
works for a week and fails for a year.

## The problems

1. **Documents live in email.** Circulars and orders arrive as attachments in
   long threads. Forwards multiply, versions diverge, and nobody can point to
   the one authoritative copy.

2. **There is no real way to search.** Finding a six month old order means
   scrolling inboxes and guessing keywords. There is no filter by branch, type
   or date, so retrieval is slow and unreliable.

3. **Files are scattered across shared drives.** Documents spread across
   personal folders and network drives with inconsistent naming. New staff have
   no idea where anything is, and nothing is truly the single source of truth.

4. **Access control is weak.** Anyone with a link or a folder path can open
   sensitive internal documents. There is no clear line between who may publish
   and who may only read.

5. **Distribution is slow and manual.** Getting a notification to the right
   branches depends on someone remembering the correct mailing list. Time
   sensitive notices reach people late, or not at all.

6. **Uploads are not checked.** Nothing verifies that an attachment is genuinely
   the document it claims to be, or that it is a reasonable size.

## Impact

- Staff waste time hunting for documents that should take seconds to find.
- Decisions get made against outdated versions.
- Sensitive material is exposed more widely than intended.
- The organisation has no dependable record of what was issued and when.

## In one sentence

There was no secure, searchable, access controlled place where every official
document could be published once and reliably found later, so the organisation
depended on email and memory for something that deserved a system of record.

That gap is what this project sets out to close. The approach is described in the
[proposed solution](PROPOSED_SOLUTION.md).
